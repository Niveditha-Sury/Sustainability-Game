## 🌟 Project Overview

**Eco-Town Builder** is an engaging, gamified educational tool designed to raise awareness about sustainability and eco-friendly decision-making. Players take on the role of a town manager, navigating real-world environmental challenges through a turn-based board game mechanic.

The core objective is to achieve **full town sustainability (100 points)** by making responsible, eco-conscious choices concerning energy, waste, water, and transport infrastructure. The game provides immediate feedback on actions, highlighting the long-term impact of sustainable versus unsustainable development.

## ✨ Key Features

* **Turn-Based Gameplay:** Players draw "Challenge Cards" and roll dice to simulate real-world events and opportunities.
* **Infrastructure Management:** Four core infrastructure areas to manage and upgrade:
    * **Energy Plant** (Power generation)
    * **Waste Center** (Recycling and management)
    * **Water Reserve** (Conservation and supply)
    * **Transport Hub** (Low-carbon mobility)
* **Sustainability Scoring:** A clear point system tracks progress toward the sustainability goal.
* **Educational Feedback:** Warnings and informative "Did You Know?" facts are provided to reinforce the educational impact of both sustainable and unsustainable choices.
* **Game Log:** A detailed log tracks all actions and events for review.

## 🎮 How to Play (Usage)

1.  **Start:** The game begins with all infrastructure in an un-upgraded state.
2.  **New Turn:** Click **'New Turn'** to draw a new Challenge Card, which presents a situation or problem.
3.  **Roll Dice:** Click **'Roll Dice for Outcome'** to determine a random factor or severity related to the challenge.
4.  **Make a Choice:** Select either **'Choose Sustainable Action'** or **'Choose Unsustainable Action'**.
5.  **Observe Impact:** The game log and sustainability score will update based on your choice, moving you closer or further away from the goal.
6.  **Win:** The game concludes when your Town Sustainability reaches **100 points**.
